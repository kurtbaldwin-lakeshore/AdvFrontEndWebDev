The Driftless story

In the spring of 1987, Walt Hollander cleared a corner of his garage in Viroqua, hung a hand-painted sign, and began fixing neighborhood bikes for a few dollars and a cup of coffee. Word travelled fast, and by summer there was a line out the garage door.

The Driftless Region escaped the last glaciers, so its ridges and coulees are steep and its roads wind to match. That is hard on legs and wonderful on a bike. Riders come from Madison, La Crosse, and Minneapolis to climb the same hills the shop sits among.

Today his daughter Pat runs the shop on Main Street. The tools are newer and the bikes are fancier, but the idea is the same: treat every rider like a neighbor, explain every repair, and send people home happier than they arrived.

Group rides leave from the shop every Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday, and Sunday. Helmets are required and all paces are welcome. Nobody gets dropped, and the coffee is always on afterward.
