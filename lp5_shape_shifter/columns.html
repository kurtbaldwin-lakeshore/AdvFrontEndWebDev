column-count: 1, then 2, then 3

The Saturday market opens before the sun is fully up. Trucks back into place along the square, tailgates drop, and crates of sweet corn, rhubarb, and early peas appear as if by magic. By seven o'clock the first customers are already weaving between the tables with tote bags over their shoulders.

Nobody hurries here. A vendor hands over a slice of cheese curd on a toothpick and asks what you plan to cook this week. Conversations stretch on while the line grows behind you, and nobody seems to mind. That unhurried pace is part of what keeps people coming back.

Around the corner, a baker slides trays of warm kringle onto a folding table. The cherry ones go first, usually before nine. Regulars know to arrive early, and newcomers learn quickly after a single disappointed trip to an empty tray.

Why columns?

Long lines of text are tiring to read. Splitting a wide block into columns keeps each line short, the same way a newspaper does. This box also refuses to be split between two columns.

Mid-morning

By ten o'clock the square is full. Kids dart between strollers, a fiddle player tunes up near the fountain, and the smell of fresh coffee drifts across the crowd. The tables that were tidy at dawn now look wonderfully picked over.

Farmers load their trucks again just after noon, tired and sunburned and happy. Whatever is left goes to the food pantry down the street. The square empties as quietly as it filled, leaving only a few stray pea pods on the pavement.

Then the whole thing starts over next week, with a different mix of crops, a few new faces, and the same unhurried pace.

column-width: let the browser decide

This block asks for columns that are at least 15rem wide and lets the browser fit as many as it can. There is no media query. The count still changes as the window grows or shrinks.

Bike trails in the Driftless Region wind through valleys and over ridges, so a ride that looks short on the map can feel long in the legs. Pack water, check your brakes, and plan for a few hills.

Most trail towns have a shop where you can pump a tire or ask about the road ahead. Locals are happy to point out the best route, and they usually know where to find the best pie.

Late afternoon is the nicest time to ride. The light turns gold, the heat backs off, and the farm stands along the way are just starting to set out their evening produce.

Properties used on this page

column-count
The number of columns. Here it goes 1, 2, then 3 at the breakpoints.
column-width
The ideal minimum width of a column. The browser works out how many fit.
column-gap
The space between columns.
column-rule
A line drawn between columns, written like a border. It takes the page's accent color.
column-span: all
Lets one element, like the "Mid-morning" heading, stretch across every column.
break-inside: avoid
Keeps an element, like the callout box, from being split between two columns.
